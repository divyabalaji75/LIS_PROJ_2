# Virginia LIS Analysis Business Glossary

This glossary translates the analysis into language suitable for research and leadership discussions. It describes observable records, not legislators' private beliefs or motives.

## Core terms

**Abstained (`A`)**  
The official LIS record indicates an abstention. It is retained but excluded from Yes/No rates and party-majority calculations.

**Bill**  
A legislative instrument identified by its official LIS bill number. The analysis uses the distinct bill count from `BILLS.CSV` as the bill universe.

**Bill description**  
The official short description published in `BILLS.CSV`. It is the final text source used for derived topic classification when no official subject or qualifying summary-derived topic is available.

**Bill history**  
The sequence of official actions published in `HISTORY.CSV`. History text supports timelines, pathway markers, and vote-to-bill relationships.

**Classification provenance**  
The evidence tier explaining how a topic was assigned. The only production values are Official LIS subject, Derived from LIS bill summary, Derived from LIS bill description, and Unclassified.

**Committee assignment**  
One legislator's membership on one committee. It is not the same as a unique person count or proof that the legislator acted on every bill handled by that committee.

**Comparable sample**  
A year-over-year row meeting documented minimum-volume requirements. Thresholds reduce the chance that a large percentage change is caused by very few observations.

**Cross-party eligible vote**  
A Yes/No member vote for which both the member's party and the comparison party have clear, strict-majority positions.

**Derived topic**  
A deterministic topic produced from official LIS summary or description text using documented rules and exclusions. It is not an official LIS subject and is not silently generated by AI.

**Directional vote**  
An official Yes (`Y`) or No (`N`) vote. Abstentions and not-voting records are non-directional.

**Exact LIS subject**  
The subject attached to the bill in `CIBillSubjects.csv`, preserved as `lis_subject_name`. This is often the more specific child subject.

**Broader LIS subject / parent subject**  
The parent resolved through `CIParentChildSubjects.csv`. It becomes `topic_name` for official-subject analysis when available, while the exact child remains auditable.

**Not voting (`X`)**  
The official record indicates that no Yes/No vote was recorded for that member. It is retained but excluded from directional rates.

**Official recorded vote**  
The `Y`, `N`, `A`, or `X` value published by LIS. A later vote statement never overwrites it.

**Official LIS subject**  
A classification directly supported by `CIBillSubjects.csv`. Bills in this tier never pass through derived topic rules.

**Other-party position**  
The strict Yes or No majority among the comparison party's directional votes in the same vote event. A tie produces no position.

**Own-party position**  
The strict Yes or No majority among the member's party's directional votes in the same vote event. A tie produces no position.

**Party break**  
A member casts Yes or No against a clear majority of their own party. It does not automatically mean the member voted with the other party.

**Party-break rate**  
Party breaks divided by directional member votes with a clear own-party majority.

**Patron / sponsor**  
A legislator officially associated with a bill in `Sponsors.csv`. Roles such as Chief Patron, Chief Co-Patron, and Co-Patron are preserved separately.

**Recorded pathway marker**  
A reproducible phrase-based indicator from official history text, such as became law, left in committee, or failed/stricken. Markers are not mutually exclusive final-disposition categories.

**Selected summary**  
The highest-maturity supported LIS summary for a bill after HTML removal. House-passed and Senate-passed summaries are treated as the same maturity for tie handling.

**Subject / topic**  
The analytical grouping attached to a bill. For official rows it is the broader parent when available, otherwise the exact subject. For derived rows it is the deterministic rule result.

**Subject source**  
Leadership-friendly wording for classification provenance.

**True cross-party vote**  
A directional member vote that both differs from the member's own-party majority and matches the other party's majority. This is deliberately narrower than party break.

**True cross-party rate**  
True cross-party votes divided by cross-party eligible votes—not all roster slots, all vote records, or all party breaks.

**Unclassified**  
A bill with no official LIS subject and no deterministic topic match from its selected summary or bill description. Unclassified is an explicit, auditable result rather than a forced guess.

**Vote event**  
A distinct official LIS vote identifier. One event may relate to multiple bills.

**Vote statement**  
Official LIS text providing context about a recorded vote. An intended Yes/No value is parsed only when explicitly stated and remains separate from the official vote.

**Vote-to-bill bridge**  
The table connecting vote events to bills through official history evidence. It handles the real one-to-many relationship without altering the canonical member-vote table.

**Voting tendency**  
A descriptive category based on the share of observed Yes and No votes within a subject after a minimum-volume threshold. It must not be described as a belief, ideology, or stance on a single policy direction.

**Yes-vote rate**  
Yes votes divided by Yes plus No votes. Abstentions and not-voting records are shown separately.

## Interpretation guardrails

- “Cross-party” describes a vote-level relationship between party majorities; it does not mean bipartisan sponsorship, compromise, persuasion, or ideological moderation.
- A high Yes rate for a subject does not necessarily mean support for one policy direction because the subject can contain opposing proposals and procedural votes.
- A session change can reflect a different agenda, membership, number of recorded votes, or data availability—not necessarily a behavioral change by itself.
- Sponsorship-vote relationships are descriptive associations, not causal findings.
- Official classifications and deterministic derived classifications should never be presented as the same source type.
